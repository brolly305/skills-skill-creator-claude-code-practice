# Kaizen Intelligence Methodology v1.0
## The Decision and Learning Process for Orlop Group Holdings
**Version 1.0 | June 2026**

---

## Overview

Kaizen Intelligence is the structured methodology by which Orlop Group Holdings — and every AI agent, human operator, and partner operating within it — makes decisions, evaluates evidence, and learns from outcomes.

The name is deliberate: Kaizen (改善) means continuous improvement. This methodology does not seek perfect decisions. It seeks decisions that are honest, traceable, and systematically improvable.

---

## Core Process — The OEDL Cycle

Every decision, large or small, moves through four phases:

```
OBSERVE → EVALUATE → DECIDE → LEARN
     ↑                              |
     └──────────────────────────────┘
              (continuous loop)
```

### Phase O — Observe

Collect the raw inputs: what is happening, what has been said, what has been measured, what has changed.

**Observe discipline:**
- Separate signal from noise before evaluation
- Record observations in their raw form before interpreting them
- Note the source and reliability of each observation
- Flag observations that conflict with prior models — these are the most valuable

**Observation types for Ohana:**
- Job site conditions (before photos, hazard scan, scope confirmation)
- Prospect signals on a cold call (tone, specific words, hesitation points)
- Review velocity and GBP ranking changes
- Gross margin per job after labor, fuel, and dump fees

**Observation types for HOL:**
- Client discovery answers (canvas gap detection)
- Campaign results (review count before/after, reactivation bookings)
- Lead source attribution (which channel produced each test drive client)
- Time-to-delivery on each component of the Local Growth System

---

### Phase E — Evaluate

Transform raw observations into structured understanding. Apply the relevant reasoning frameworks (see Framework Library). Assign confidence levels. Identify assumptions.

**Evaluate discipline:**
- Apply at least one adversarial lens to every significant evaluation (red team, steelman, premortem)
- Name every load-bearing assumption explicitly
- Assign confidence tier to the conclusion (Tier 1-5 per Epistemic Charter)
- Identify what evidence would change the conclusion

**Evaluation questions:**
- What does this observation actually mean? (vs. what does it feel like it means)
- What alternative explanations exist?
- What is the best argument against the current interpretation?
- What would we need to observe to be more confident?

---

### Phase D — Decide

Select the action based on the evaluated evidence. Record the reasoning. Set revision triggers.

**Decide discipline:**
- Prefer reversible over irreversible decisions when evidence is incomplete
- Commit to the smallest version of the decision that tests the hypothesis
- Record: what was decided, when, why, what alternatives were rejected and why
- Set explicit revision triggers: "We will revisit this decision if X happens"

**Decision types by reversibility:**

| Reversibility | Examples | Required evidence threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Fully reversible | NEPQ script variation, blog post topic, GBP category | Low — test and observe |
| Partially reversible | Pricing structure, campaign type, referral ask format | Medium — some evidence base |
| Largely irreversible | Equipment financing, LLC formation, partnership agreement | High — documented demand and margin |
| Irreversible | Entity dissolution, trademark filing, major legal commitment | Very high — professional review required |

---

### Phase L — Learn

Document the outcome. Update the organizational model. Feed the Conformance Corpus.

**Learn discipline:**
- Every decision with a meaningful outcome generates a lessons learned entry
- Updates are dated and attributed to the evidence that drove them
- Models are updated at minimum cadence (weekly for operational, monthly for strategic)
- Negative outcomes are documented with the same rigor as positive ones — they are more valuable

**Learning outputs:**
- Conformance Corpus entry (for significant decisions)
- Updated confidence level in the relevant model
- Revised pricing, process, or script (for operational learning)
- Knowledge base update (for strategic learning)

## Decision Lifecycle

Every decision moves through these states:

```
IDENTIFIED → UNDER EVALUATION → COMMITTED → ACTIVE → CLOSED → ARCHIVED
                                    ↓
                              (revision trigger fires)
                                    ↓
                            UNDER REVISION → RECOMMITTED
```

**State definitions:**

**IDENTIFIED:** A decision need has been recognized. The observation phase begins.

**UNDER EVALUATION:** Evidence is being collected and frameworks are being applied. No commitment yet.

**COMMITTED:** A decision has been made and recorded. Revision triggers are set.

**ACTIVE:** The decision is being implemented. Observations about implementation quality are being collected.

**CLOSED:** The decision cycle is complete. Outcome is documented. Learning phase begins.

**ARCHIVED:** The decision and its outcome are stored in the Conformance Corpus for future reference.

**UNDER REVISION:** A revision trigger has fired. The decision is being reconsidered with new evidence.

## Reasoning Philosophy

Kaizen Intelligence is grounded in three philosophical commitments:

**1. Calibrated honesty over confident performance**
We would rather be correctly uncertain than falsely confident. A low-confidence decision that is labeled honestly is more useful than a high-confidence decision that is wrong.

**2. Process over intuition — until intuition is earned**
Intuition is pattern recognition built from experience. Before that experience is built, process produces better outcomes than intuition. After experience is built, process validates and sharpens intuition. The NEPQ memory palace — encoding the conversation flow spatially — is an example of building process until it becomes intuition.

**3. Systems thinking over event thinking**
Every outcome is produced by a system, not by an event. A bad cold call is not a failure — it is a data point in a system. A missed job booking is not bad luck — it is a signal that a part of the intake-to-close system needs adjustment. Think in systems. Intervene at the system level.

## Minimum Viable Application

For operators who need a quick-start version:

**Before any significant decision, ask:**
1. What do I actually know? (observation)
2. What does it mean? (evaluation)
3. What am I assuming? (name it)
4. What would change my conclusion? (revision trigger)
5. What is the smallest commitment that tests this? (decide)
6. What will I learn from the outcome? (learning plan)

**After any significant outcome, record:**
1. What was decided and when
2. What happened
3. What the evidence said vs. what actually occurred
4. What updates to make to the relevant model
