# Five Rings Scroll — output template for Mode: Build

Follow this structure. Fill every section with the user's specifics; a section that
could belong to someone else's endeavor isn't finished. Keep the headings — they're
the spine of the document and let the user return to one ring later.

Length: aim for something a person will actually read and act on. Roughly 800–1,500
words for a normal endeavor. If a ring genuinely has little in it (no live opponent,
say), write two honest sentences rather than padding it — principle 9 applies to
your own output.

---

## Template

```markdown
# Five Rings Scroll: [endeavor, named plainly]

**The endeavor:** [one unglamorous sentence — what this actually is]
**Winning looks like:** [a state someone else could verify, with a number or a date]
**Written on assumptions:** [only if you had to assume; name them so they can be corrected]

---

## 地 Earth — the ground

**What this is, honestly.** [Strip the framing. If they said "AI-powered wellness
platform," say what it is: a subscription app that reminds people to drink water.]

**The timber.** [Inventory what they actually have — people, money, time, skills,
assets, unfair advantages — and place each where its grain fits. Name at least one
thing they're using in the wrong place. Include what they don't have.]

**The tools, and whether they're sharp.** [The concrete instruments: the skill,
the list, the software, the relationship. Which need sharpening before use.]

**The layout.** [Sequence and proportion — what gets built first, what gets done
fast, what gets done carefully, what doesn't get built at all.]

---

## 水 Water — the form

**The everyday stance.** [The default operating posture that must survive a bad
week. Combat stance = everyday stance.]

**The techniques to drill.** [Three to five, no more. The small vocabulary they'll
actually own, not a catalogue.]

**The training regimen.** [Cadence: daily / weekly / per cycle. What they
repeatedly do. This is principle 2 made concrete — a schedule, not a reading list.]

**When to drop the form.** [Attitude-no-attitude: the conditions under which this
plan should be abandoned rather than defended.]

---

## 火 Fire — the engagement

**The opponent.** [Named. A competitor, the market, entropy, their own avoidance.
If there is genuinely no opponent, say so and keep this ring short.]

**The ground.** [Where the contest happens and how to choose ground that favors
them — venue, terms, comparison, metric.]

**The initiative.** [Which of the three — ken no sen (attack first), tai no sen
(receive and counter), tai tai no sen (meet it at inception) — and why this one.]

**The tactics.** [Two or three from references/fire.md that genuinely fit, each
translated into a specific move: who does what, when, and the likely response.]

**The first exchange.** [What actually happens in week one, and what "collapse"
would look like if it starts — so they recognize it and press.]

---

## 風 Wind — the other schools

[Two or three rival approaches, characterized concretely. For each:]

**[Approach / competitor]** — What they do: [specific]. What they believe wins:
[the underlying bet]. Where it fails: [the conditions]. Take: [what to steal].
Refuse: [what's ornament].

**What we do that they don't.** [State it plainly. If the answer is "nothing,"
that is the most valuable line in this scroll — say it.]

---

## 空 Void — what remains

**Genuinely unknown.** [Named unknowns, not "the future is uncertain."]

**Only learnable by contact.** [Where more analysis has zero marginal value, and
the cheap test that resolves it.]

**What you're pretending to know.** [The estimate presented as fact, the number
that came from a feeling. Usually the most valuable sentence here.]

**First move, and the checkpoint.** [What they do first. When they come back and
look at it.]

---

## The Dokkōdō for this work

[Three to seven terse precepts, specific to this person's actual temptations.
Declarative. Checkable at the end of a week. At least one should forbid something
they like doing.]

1. [precept]
2. [precept]
3. [precept]
```

---

## Notes on filling it

- **Earth before everything.** If the honest one-sentence definition is hard to
  write, that difficulty *is* the finding — surface it rather than smoothing it.
- **Fire is where this goes wrong.** The temptation is to tour the tactic catalogue.
  Two or three that fit, each cashed out into a real move. "Injure the corners" is
  not advice; "take the self-serve tier the incumbent stopped supporting" is.
- **Wind requires actual knowledge.** If you don't know the user's field well enough
  to characterize rivals concretely, ask rather than generalize.
- **Void must be the most concrete section**, not the most poetic. If it ends in
  serenity it has failed; if it ends in a test scheduled for a specific week it has
  worked.
- **The Dokkōdō is what survives.** People forget analysis and keep the rules on
  the wall. Spend real effort on the precepts.
